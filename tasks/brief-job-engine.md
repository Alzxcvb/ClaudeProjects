# Brief: Job Engine

Raw input date: 2026-09-07. Source: Alex's own idea, prompted by a Threads post pointing at inclimate.com, One Green Thing, and Project Drawdown.

## Definition of done

The system runs unattended for 7 consecutive mornings, and on each of those mornings a Telegram report arrives showing new scored matches, what was submitted or is waiting for approval, and, where inbox access exists, any employer replies.

Throughput floor, because seven empty reports would otherwise satisfy the sentence above while nothing works: at least five of the seven mornings must produce a qualifying report, and at least one application must be confirmed by an employer confirmation page or a confirmation email. A collector returning zero rows every morning is a failed run, not a completed one.

A qualifying report means: at least one new scored match was stored that morning, and the run was not degraded for a disqualifying reason. Degradation reasons are stored individually on the report row rather than collapsed into one boolean, because they are not equivalent. Disqualifying: the browser stage did not run, or a lock refusal blocked the run. "Did not run" means the stage failed preflight or aborted with an error. A stage that starts, finds zero items to work on, and completes cleanly HAS run, and that morning still qualifies. This distinction matters more than it looks: on a morning where dedupe blocks everything or nothing clears a floor, the stage legitimately has no work, and reading that as a failure would mark most mornings non qualifying for no real reason. Not disqualifying: the model call cap tripped and the run degraded to fewer candidates, which is the system working as designed rather than failing. Without this split, a morning where everything worked but the budget cap engaged would count as a failure, which is wrong.

Said plainly so nobody mistakes it later: this floor is a smoke test proving the pipeline works end to end. It is not a performance target. Alex asked for applying daily at volume, and whether the system achieves that is measured after the seven days, not by this check.

Clock, split into two groups because some items logically cannot clear before the build exists. Day 0 waits only on the pre build gates. The pre live send gates must clear by day 7, and until they do the system runs in dry run.

- **Pre build gates, these start the clock:** item 1 the resume and corpus allowlist, item 2 the free tier provider key, item 9 the Telegram bot token, item 13 the decision about keeping the Mac awake.
- **Pre live send gates, needed before anything is submitted but not before building:** item 4 the phone number, item 6 the lane 2 floor confirmation, item 7 the submission terms sign off, item 8 the answer bank, item 10 Alex reading a dry run and turning `DRY_RUN` off. Item 10 obviously cannot happen before there is a build to produce a dry run, which is why it sits here rather than at day 0.
- **Non gating, their absence just removes a feature:** item 3 inbox access removes the reply tracker, item 5 the target city list removes the relocation branch, item 12 the Adzuna key removes one source.
- **Item 11 is cleared.** Alex set the remote climate floor at $150,000 on 2026-09-07, so lane 3 runs on both branches and nothing here is deferred. The relocation branch alone still waits on target cities, item 5, which is non gating. Build runs days 1 to 7 and must be live by day 7. The 7 unattended mornings then run days 8 to 14. Earliest honest completion is day 14 after the blockers clear. The 7 day box Alex set covers the build only.

## Scope

In:
- Three goal lanes, priority order as stated: (1) part time remote, mostly automatable; (2) full time remote, automatable, check in only; (3) climate full time, mission driven, high pay.
- Daily ingestion from free public job sources with no auth: Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, and Recruitee job board APIs, plus Remotive, RemoteOK, and Arbeitnow. Adzuna is listed separately because it is not no auth: it requires a registered app id and app key, and its free tier limits were never verified. - Higher education and public sector sources, which the list above does not cover and which lane 1 depends on. Alex's own top priority archetype is an online community college astronomy instructor, and those postings live on HigherEdJobs, EDJOIN, the California community college district sites, and the CCC Registry, none of which sit on the five startup ATS platforms. Without these, lane 1 returns almost none of what he actually described. Verify each source's terms and free access before wiring it, the same way the others were verified.
- A seed query list derived from Alex's two archetypes, not from generic titles: "instructor", "adjunct", "online instructor" for lane 1, and "analyst", "data analyst", "data", "reporting", "business intelligence" for lane 2. His friend's claim was that roles with "analyst" or "data" in the title are the automatable ones. Note that such roles at older non tech employers frequently sit on Workday, Taleo or iCIMS, which v1 excludes, so lane 2 coverage is genuinely reduced and that tradeoff is accepted rather than hidden.
- Climate boards with no public API. CHECKED 2026-09-07 and two are dropped: Climatebase and Terra.do both explicitly forbid automated reading, Terra.do's terms saying "Use, launch, develop, or distribute any automated system, including without limitation, any spider, robot, cheat utility, scraper... that accesses the Site" and Climatebase carrying the same clause, so neither may be read programmatically. That is the terms check doing its job rather than a setback. Climate employers are still reachable, and reachable better, through their own Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby boards, which are public, permitted, and where the application actually gets submitted anyway. The remaining boards, inclimate.com, ClimateTechList and Work on Climate, have permissive robots files and no terms found forbidding it, and are read with Playwright connected over CDP to the job engine's own dedicated Chrome profile on its own debug port. Never the Prospector profile on port 9222, for the reasons in Constraints. Verify each of these boards' terms and free access before wiring it, exactly as required for the higher education sources above. This is not a formality: Indeed was cut from this brief because its terms prohibit robots and scrapers, and Terra.do and Climatebase are gated community products that quite possibly prohibit the same thing. Applying one standard to Indeed and a looser one here would be incoherent, and these are the boards Alex most wants to stay welcome on. If a board's terms fail the check, drop that board and reach its listings through the employer's own ATS board instead, which is public. Whether automated submission through that surface is permitted is a separate question, checked separately, below.
- Per posting scores: lane match, automation potential (how much of the listed work is repeatable and tool driven), pay against the lane floor, stated hours, effective hourly rate, and a company reputation read.
- Hours are a first class field, not an afterthought, because both archetypes Alex gave are about hours rather than pay. The astronomy instructor earns about $3,000 a month for under five hours a week; the analyst earned $60,000 a year for about an hour a day. What he is actually optimizing on lanes 1 and 2 is money per hour actually worked, so a posting stores stated hours, carries an hours unknown flag when the posting is silent, and gets an effective hourly rate computed from pay divided by stated hours. Automation potential then adjusts that: a role that scores high on automation is worth more per nominal hour because fewer of those hours stay real. Hours unknown routes like pay unknown only for figures that cannot be floor checked without stated hours; see the pay normalization rule below. Where it does route that way, cash lanes may still auto submit and the climate lane always queues. It never authorizes skipping a floor check that the posting's own stated figures make possible.
- Pay normalization rule, so postings quoting hourly, monthly and annual figures can be compared. Normalize using stated hours per week where the posting gives one, and where it does not, treat a full time posting as 40 hours a week and 52 weeks a year and mark the derived figure as estimated. Two rules that stop this breaking the floor checks:
  - Compare each posting against its lane floor in that floor's own native unit, on unrounded values. The part time floor is monthly, the two full time floors are annual. Normalizing everything to a rounded monthly integer would turn $60,001 into $5,000 and fail this document's own boundary test.
  - The hours unknown flag blocks only pay figures that would have to be derived through hours. A posting stating its pay in the floor's own native unit, monthly for lane 1 or annual for lanes 2 and 3, is compared against that floor whether or not hours are stated. So a part time posting saying "$1,500 a month, hours unspecified" is rejected by the monthly floor, not waved through as pay unknown. It does not suppress a rate the posting already states either: an hourly quoted posting has a known effective hourly rate whether or not weekly hours are given. And automation potential adjusts the desirability score only, never the figure compared against a pay floor. A job does not become better paid because more of it can be automated.
- Company reputation as a written summary where every claim carries a source URL and a verbatim quote, or is omitted entirely. Not a scraped numeric rating: Glassdoor shut its public API and there is no free legal programmatic path to Glassdoor, Blind, Comparably, or Levels.fyi ratings, and that limitation is stated in the report rather than papered over. Permitted sources are named in config, and summarizing search result snippets is forbidden. This is not a theoretical worry: summarizer prose passed off as a source is a mistake already made and corrected once, and a hallucinated culture claim here would steer Alex away from a real job or into a bad one.
- Custom resume and cover letter drafted per application from Alex's real experience corpus, behind a traceability gate. Every factual claim in a generated document (employer, title, dates, degree, licence, tool, metric) must map to a specific line in an allowlisted source file. This is enforced in code, not by asking the model nicely in a prompt. A sentence that fails the trace is dropped, and if the drop guts the document the application routes to the approval queue instead of sending. These documents go out under Alex's real name to real employers and cannot be recalled, and a fabricated credential found at reference check ends the hire and follows him. This is the same rule he already applies to published claims, pointed at the thing the system produces.
- Submission direct to the company: the employer's own site or its hosted ATS application page.
- Cash lanes (1 and 2) auto submit. Climate lane (3) goes to a Telegram approval queue that Alex confirms before anything sends. Note on provenance: the auto versus approve split is a default adopted from the recommendation offered to Alex. His own answer addressed where to apply, company sites rather than LinkedIn, not whether to auto submit. His original ask said "automatically apply," so the split is a reasonable reading, but it was not his explicit selection.
- Daily Telegram report, plus a read only tracker over the dedicated job hunt inbox that surfaces employer replies and flags applications that have gone quiet past a threshold. Assumption stated rather than resolved silently: Alex asked for "follow ups with different companies," which could mean tracking follow ups or sending them. v1 reads and flags only. Sending outbound follow up messages to employers is explicitly out of scope for v1 and listed below.
- Alex rates report lines. This is in the literal ask: "I would be able to prioritize what jobs sound really good, what jobs sound not as good." Each match in the daily report carries a good or not good button. Ratings are stored and the most recent rated examples are fed into the next day's scoring prompt. The window size starts at 20, which is an arbitrary starting value rather than a tuned one, and should be adjusted once there is real rating data, so the system learns his taste rather than only his filters. Without this the cash lanes submit with no signal from him at all.

Submission safety controls, all six required before any live send, each with its own file, step and test rather than a line of prose:
- `MAX_SUBMISSIONS_PER_DAY` as a config constant, checked before every send.
- Deduplication on a uniqueness key of company plus normalized role title, with a cooldown window, applied across boards and across days. The same posting appears on Greenhouse, Adzuna, Remotive and RemoteOK at once, and without this the system sends one company several applications on day one and several more on day two, which is the fastest route to being blocklisted on Alex's real name, email and phone.
- `DRY_RUN` defaults to on. It produces every artifact, the custom resume, the cover letter and the filled form values, and sends nothing. It stays on until Alex has read a real dry run output and turns it off himself.
- A stop file the runner checks immediately before each submission, so a run in flight can be halted without killing the process.
- `MAX_MODEL_CALLS_PER_RUN` as a per tier config constant, checked before each model batch. This replaced a dollar cap on 2026-09-07 when Alex ruled out all API spend; see the inference engine section. Haiku triage across thousands of postings plus Sonnet writing per application is otherwise unbounded spend, which matters more than usual because the reason for building this is that money is tight. On hitting the cap the run degrades to fewer candidates and says so in the report, rather than overspending or dying silently.
- Full payload capture per application, written before the send, never after. Store the rendered resume, the cover letter, and every form field value against the application row. Without this a bad submission cannot be audited after the fact, and auditing outward facing sends is exactly what Alex's blast radius rule requires.

Application answer bank. Real forms require work authorization, country of residence, willingness to relocate, salary expectation, whether he previously worked there, notice period, references, and EEO, veteran and disability self identification. Several of these are legally attested. The system never guesses any of them. Alex supplies a fixed answer file, and any required field with no mapping stops that application and routes it to the approval queue. Related, and this needs care because it is easy to get backwards: Alex is living overseas, and many United States remote roles require the employee to reside in the United States. Filter on residency at the time of hire, not on work authorization, and exempt the relocation branch of the climate lane, where being willing to move is the whole point. Do not filter on work authorization at all: the brief does not establish Alex's citizenship or authorization status, and guessing would delete most of the funnel for someone who is in fact authorized. His authorization status goes in the answer bank as a fact he states, and is used to answer forms, not to filter.

Missing salary rule, since most postings do not state pay and a strict floor would drop most of the funnel: a posting with no stated salary passes to scoring carrying a pay unknown flag. Cash lane roles may auto submit on unknown pay, because volume there is cheap and the downside is small. Climate lane roles with unknown pay always route to the approval queue regardless of score. This is a stated assumption, not something Alex specified.

Out (v1 explicitly does not do this):

## Inference engine under the zero spend budget

Stated as a design direction on 2026-09-07, pending the free tier research confirming the middle tier. Once inference is capacity rather than tokens, the architecture changes shape, so this is written down rather than left to an executing model to improvise.

A local Python script cannot call the Anthropic Messages API on a Max subscription, and it must not try. Anthropic's own support documentation states that a paid Claude subscription "doesn't include access to the Claude API or Console," and its consumer terms as updated in February 2026 restrict subscription OAuth to Claude Code and claude.ai rather than to arbitrary scripts. Borrowing subscription credentials for a script is therefore both technically wrong and against terms, and is forbidden here.

What does run on the $100 subscription is Claude Code itself. So the model in the loop is Claude Code, scheduled as a routine, invoking this repo's code as its tools, rather than a script making API calls.

This is explicitly permitted. Claude Code's legal documentation states that OAuth authentication "is intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription plans and is designed to support ordinary use of Claude Code and other native Anthropic applications." Routines are a native Claude Code feature and draw on the same subscription limits as an interactive session. The prohibition is on third party tools borrowing OAuth, not on Anthropic's own product.

On the billing trap, corrected after a second research pass. A separate monthly Agent SDK credit was announced in June 2026 but was paused and is not in effect as of September 2026, so `claude -p` and Agent SDK usage currently draw on ordinary subscription limits with no separate balance and no rollover. If Anthropic reactivates it, the documented behaviour is a hard stop: "If usage credits aren't enabled, Agent SDK requests stop until your credit refreshes." So the standing rule for this build is that usage credits are never enabled on Alex's account. That single setting is what keeps a zero spend budget at zero. Re check this before the first live run, because it is the kind of policy that moves.

What this costs is capacity rather than dollars: every tier 2 call competes with Alex's own Claude Code use for the same subscription limits. That is the real budget here, and it is why tiers 0 and 1 exist, to keep tier 2 small.

The ChatGPT subscription at $20 a month is a red herring for this build. ChatGPT Plus includes no OpenAI API credits and no programmatic access. Do not go looking for a way to use it.

Three tiers, cheapest first, because subscription capacity is finite and Alex uses it for everything else:

- **Tier 0, pure code, no model at all.** The free ATS APIs return structured fields, so lane keyword match, the remote flag, residency eligibility, and the pay floor checks are ordinary code. This is where the bulk of 400 daily postings gets cut. Every model call avoided here is capacity kept.
- **Tier 1, shallow classification on what survives.** Lane fit and a first pass automation potential read. High volume, low judgment, so it belongs on a genuinely free tier rather than on the subscription. Provider limits could NOT be verified, and this is worth stating plainly rather than papering over. Groq publishes its free tier table behind a client side render, so a fetch returns only audio model rows; an earlier research pass read those rows and reported 100 requests a day, which is not a text model figure and is not to be trusted in either direction. Google publishes no fixed free tier numbers at all and shows per account limits inside AI Studio. Both therefore require an account before the real limits are knowable, which makes this a signup step rather than a research step.

The design must not assume a number. Tier 1 volume is bounded by `MAX_MODEL_CALLS_PER_RUN['tier1']`, degrades to fewer candidates rather than failing when the provider rate limits, and paces itself rather than bursting. The first thing to do after the account exists is read the real limits from the console and write them, with the date, into the README.

Two consequences follow. First, tier 0 carries more weight than originally assumed: the harder the code filters cut, the less tier 1 has to survive an unknown ceiling, so effort spent on tier 0 filtering is the highest value work in this build. Second, a local model is promoted from escape hatch to genuine fallback, because it is the only tier 1 option whose limits are known in advance, being none. Build the tier behind one interface so a provider can be swapped when a free tier changes, which they do.
- **Tier 2, deep scoring, company notes, and the writing.** Low volume, roughly 25 deep scores and 8 letters a day, and the highest judgment. This runs through Claude Code on the subscription.

Two consequences that must not be missed:

- `MAX_DAILY_TOKEN_SPEND_USD` is now meaningless, since there are no dollars to cap. It is replaced by `MAX_MODEL_CALLS_PER_RUN`, set per tier, checked before each batch, degrading to fewer candidates exactly as the dollar cap did. The acceptance check moves with it. A control whose units no longer exist is dead prose with a test bolted on.
- If the routine runs in the cloud, the "Mac must be awake" item stops gating ingestion, scoring and reporting. It does not disappear, because the submission step still needs the local Chrome. The brief must say which stages move before the plan is reconciled.

Zapier is not a fit here and should not be reached for. Its free tier is 100 tasks a month on two step Zaps, against a workload of roughly 400 postings a day. Alex's standing preference for existing no code tools is right in general and simply does not survive this volume.

Local models are a first class tier 1 fallback, not merely an escape hatch, precisely because no free tier ceiling could be verified. On Apple Silicon, budget roughly 0.6 GB of RAM per billion parameters, so a 16 GB machine runs a 7B to 9B model at Q4_K_M. That is adequate for tier 1 classification, which is a short structured judgement per posting, and not adequate for the writing, which stays at tier 2 regardless. Since the run is already local and already needs the Mac awake for the submit stage, a local tier 1 adds no new infrastructure requirement.

## Why build rather than adopt an existing tool

Alex's standing rule is to reach for an existing free tool before writing custom code, so here is the check rather than an assertion. This section was re checked against primary sources on 2026-09-07 and the first version of it was partly wrong. The corrections are recorded rather than smoothed over, because this is the section that justifies spending seven days.

- **Simplify.jobs auto submits.** The earlier claim that it is autofill and tracking only is CONTRADICTED by its own site, which shows an "Auto Apply" toggle switched on and a queue of jobs waiting to apply. That was the cleanest of the original disqualifications and it does not hold.
- **LazyApply is not LinkedIn bound, and is far cheaper than reported.** $99 a year for 15 applications a day, $149 for 150 a day, $999 for 1,500. It supports Greenhouse, Dice, Indeed, ZipRecruiter, LinkedIn Easy Apply and direct company sites. The earlier reading of "$99 a month, LinkedIn driven" was wrong on both counts.
- **Sonara** is operating and appears platform agnostic rather than LinkedIn bound. It publishes no pricing page.
- **AIHawk could not be verified at all.** The repository returns 404, so its licence, its claimed 30,000 stars and its supposed LinkedIn-only design are all unconfirmed. Do not cite it as a justification for anything.
- **Jobright.ai** has a free tier advertising one click autofill across major ATS platforms. Whether it truly auto submits, what its free tier actually allows, and whether any data export exists all sit behind a login and are unverified.

So the honest position: the original claim that every incumbent is LinkedIn bound was wrong, and several of these could plausibly be applying for Alex today, one of them for under $150 a year.

What did survive the check is the actual wedge, and it survived cleanly. No tool found, free or paid, scores how automatable a posted job would be for the person who takes it. That is the one thing this entire build exists to do, and it is the thing both of Alex's archetypes turn on, since the astronomy instructor and the analyst were both stories about hours rather than pay. Nor does any incumbent carry three separate goal lanes with four different pay floors, feed his own good and not good ratings back into scoring, or leave him owning the data.

That is a narrower justification than this brief started with, and he should know it before seven days go in. The fallback if he would rather not build: LazyApply at $99 a year does the mechanical applying, and the automation scoring is the part he gives up.
