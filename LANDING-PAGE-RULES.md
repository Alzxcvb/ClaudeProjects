# Landing Page Rules of Thumb

Quick reference for two modes: (1) fake-door test to validate demand before building, and (2) real offer page to convert qualified visitors.

---

## Mode 1: Fake Door Test

**Use when:** You have a new idea and want to know if anyone actually wants it before spending time building it.
**Goal:** Email signups or waitlist clicks. NOT a sale. NOT a demo. NOT a pitch deck.
**Time to launch:** Under 1 hour.

### Fake Door Structure (minimum viable)

Only 3 sections. Nothing else.

**1. Hook (above the fold)**
One headline. One subheadline. One CTA. That is the whole page.

- Headline: State the outcome the person gets. Not what your thing is. What they walk away with.
- Subheadline: Who it's for and one specific pain it solves. One sentence.
- CTA: "Join the waitlist" or "Notify me when it's ready." NOT "Buy now." NOT "Book a call."

**2. Problem (2-3 bullets)**
Name the pain they already feel. Use their words, not marketing words.

**3. Email capture form**
Name + email. Nothing else. No phone. No company. No "tell us about yourself."
- Button copy: "Get early access" or "Notify me" or "Reserve my spot"
- Subtext: "Free. No spam. Unsubscribe anytime."

### Fake Door Rules

- No nav bar. None. Nav links are exit paths that cost you conversions.
- No pricing page. You have nothing to sell yet.
- No long copy. If you are writing a paragraph, you are already overthinking it.
- No video unless it is already made. A placeholder "watch the demo" is a lie.
- Plain background + 1 accent color. Dark or light. Not both. No gradients.
- Mobile-first. Write the headline, test it on your phone. If it wraps past 3 lines, it is too long.

### Go / Kill Threshold

Launch with a specific traffic source (LinkedIn post, Twitter thread, Threads post, Reddit comment, email to your list).

| Metric | Threshold | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Email capture rate | > 10% of unique visitors | Demand signal. Build a waitlist page and drive more traffic. |
| Email capture rate | 5 to 10% | Weak signal. Test a different headline before giving up. |
| Email capture rate | < 5% | Kill the idea or completely reframe the problem statement. |

**Decision rule:** 100 unique visitors is the minimum sample to read. If you cannot get 100 visitors in 48 hours from your own channels, your distribution problem is bigger than your product problem.

### Fake Door Headline Templates

Pick one. Fill in your offer.

1. "[Outcome they want] — without [thing they hate doing]"
2. "[Hard thing] in [timeframe], even if [biggest objection]"
3. "Finally: [specific thing that has never existed before but should]"
4. "Get [dream result] like [credible example], without [barrier they assume exists]"

**Test:** Read the headline to someone who has never heard of your idea. Can they explain what the product does? If not, rewrite.

### Fake Door Checklist (before pushing)

- [ ] Headline names the outcome, not the tool
- [ ] Subheadline names the ICP (one specific person, not "businesses")
- [ ] No nav
- [ ] Single CTA, single color
- [ ] Form captures name + email only
- [ ] Mobile renders cleanly
- [ ] Page loads in under 2 seconds
- [ ] You have a traffic source ready to send to it the day it goes live

## Mode 2: Real Offer Page

**Use when:** You have validated demand and a real product or service to sell.
**Goal:** One specific conversion action (book a call, buy now, start trial).
**Time to launch:** Whatever it takes to get it right.

### Section Order (evidence-based)

This order is not arbitrary. Each section earns the next one.

**1. Hero (above the fold)**
Job: Confirm you are in the right place. Communicate the core value in one sentence. One action to take.

Required elements:
- Eyebrow: Short context label (who this is for, or what this is)
- Headline: Specific outcome for a specific person. Not clever. Clear.
- Subheadline: How it works, who it is for, one key differentiator
- CTA: One button. Primary color. Benefit-driven label, not generic ("Get X" not "Submit")
- Photo: Real photo of you if it is a personal brand/service. Real product screenshot if it is a tool. Not stock.
- Micro social proof: One number, one logo, one star rating near the button

Headline structure that works:
"[Specific Outcome] [for Specific Audience], [in Timeframe], [without Objection]"

**2. Problem / Stakes**
Job: Make the visitor feel understood before they will consider your solution.

- Name the pain in their words, not yours
- Describe the downstream consequences of doing nothing
- 2 to 4 specific "pain points" in short blocks (not a paragraph wall)
- End with: "The gap was never [the obvious thing]. It is [the real thing]."

**3. Solution / How It Works**
Job: Explain what you do and how it works at a high level. Not features. The mechanism.

- 3-step numbered process is the clearest format
- Each step = one sentence on what happens
- No jargon. If a 10-year-old cannot understand step 1, rewrite it.

**4. What You Get (Value Stack)**
Job: Make the exchange explicit. Remove all ambiguity about what happens when they buy.

- List every deliverable
- Translate each item: not "60-min session" but "60 minutes where we do X, so you leave with Y"
- Show the anchor value vs. your price
- Guarantee goes here or immediately after

**5. Proof**
Job: Remove doubt that this works and that you are the right person.

Order of proof strength (use what you have):
1. Named testimonials with a specific outcome ("went from X to Y in Z weeks")
2. Stats with context ("50+ setups, 300+ trained")
3. Recognizable credentials or press (Network School, named publication)
4. Your own transformation story (Hormozi: "use yourself" when no client proof yet)

Proof placement rule: at minimum, one micro-proof signal in the hero AND a full proof section before pricing.

**6. Objection / FAQ**
Job: Destroy the last reasons not to act.

Standard objections for a solo consultant / service offer:
- "I am not technical enough" — answer: you do not need to be, here is what I do
- "Will this work for my business?" — answer: here is the exact type of person this works for
- "What if it does not work?" — answer: guarantee language, stated plainly
- "What does it cost after this?" — answer: state clearly, no hidden next-step costs
- "What do I need to prepare?" — answer: nothing, here is what you bring

Use an AI to surface more: paste your full page copy and ask "what would a skeptical reader still wonder after reading this?" Add the best 3 to 5 answers.

**7. Final CTA**
Job: One last on-ramp for visitors who read the whole page.

- Repeat the core offer in 1 sentence
- Same button as the hero, same color, same label
- Nothing new here. No new info. Just the action.

### CTA Rules

**The single most important rule:** One conversion action per page.

- "Book a call" and "Buy now" on the same page = choice paralysis = fewer of both
- Repeat the button at multiple points (hero, after proof, bottom) but it always does the same thing
- Secondary options (free call, email list) go below the primary as text links, never as buttons
- Label the button with the benefit: "Book your Quick Start" beats "Book a call" beats "Submit"
- High contrast color. The button should be the most visually obvious thing on the page.

### Copy Rules

**Headline:**
- Outcome first. Not your product name. Not a tagline.
- If the person reading has never heard of your product, can they still understand what they get? If not, rewrite.
- Run the 5-second test: show to someone unfamiliar, cover, ask "what does this do?"

**Body copy:**
- Every "we" statement should be rewritten as a "you" statement
- Features tell. Benefits sell. "Claude installed" tells. "Claude installed, configured, and actually running your specific workflow when we hang up" sells.
- Specificity converts. "8+ hours a week" beats "a lot of time." "50+ setups" beats "many setups."
- VOC (voice of customer): use the words your clients use when they describe their problem, not the words you use when you explain your solution

**What to never put on a landing page:**
- Nav bar on a campaign page (removes exit paths, yes, but also splits focus)
- Multiple competing CTAs as buttons
- Placeholder testimonials ("your testimonial here" is negative social proof)
- Stock photos of models or generic office scenes
- Countdown timers that reset on page reload (fake scarcity destroys trust forever)
- "We are a leading provider of..." (no one cares, translate to outcome)
- Long paragraphs without bold/bullet structure (F-pattern reading means they skip it)

### Proof Rules

- Real client photos convert 35% better than no photo next to a testimonial
- A specific result ("cut my research time from 6 hours to 45 minutes") converts better than a feeling ("Alex is great!")
- Social proof near the CTA button specifically lifts conversions 14 to 68% (place one stat or micro-quote right next to or below your primary CTA button)
- No proof yet? Use: stats (volume numbers), credentials (recognizable institutions), your own story as the transformation

### Hormozi Value Equation (run every offer through this)

Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay + Effort and Sacrifice)

To raise perceived value:
- Raise Dream Outcome: name the specific vivid end state
- Raise Perceived Likelihood: add proof, guarantee, credentials
- Reduce Time Delay: show speed ("running by end of call")
- Reduce Effort: "I do the technical part, you watch"

Your guarantee is the single most powerful lever on Perceived Likelihood. Use it.

### Mobile and Performance Rules

- Above the fold captures 57% of page viewing time. If the hero does not convert, nothing below it fixes it.
- Page load under 2 seconds. Every second of delay costs roughly 7% of conversions.
- Test your page on a real phone before calling it done. Not browser devtools. A real phone.
- CTA button must be thumb-friendly on mobile (minimum 44px height, full width or close to it).

### Section Checklist (before pushing)

- [ ] Hero headline survives the 5-second test (someone unfamiliar can explain what you do)
- [ ] Single CTA button, no competing actions
- [ ] Real photo of you or real product screenshot (no stock)
- [ ] At least one proof signal above the fold
- [ ] Value stack translates every deliverable into a visitor outcome
- [ ] Guarantee is stated plainly and prominently
- [ ] FAQ covers: "will this work for me?", "what if it doesn't work?", "what does it cost after?"
- [ ] No placeholder testimonials
- [ ] No nav links that pull focus off the CTA
- [ ] OG image set (og:image, og:title, og:description, twitter:card) so share cards render
- [ ] Mobile renders cleanly at 375px width
- [ ] Page loads under 2 seconds

## Quick Reference: Psychological Principles

| Principle | What it does | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Social proof | People follow what others do | Numbers, testimonials, logos near CTA |
| Authority | Credentials reduce skepticism | Named institutions, press, certifications |
| Reciprocity | Give value first, ask second | Free guide, free call, free audit before paid |
| Scarcity | Real limits drive action | "3 Quick Start slots left this week" — only if TRUE |
| Commitment | Small yeses lead to big yeses | Free call before $200 session before retainer |
| Liking | People buy from people they like | Real photo, conversational tone, shared identity with ICP |
| Risk reversal | Remove the downside of being wrong | Money-back guarantee, "or I keep working free" |

The Fogg Behavior Model diagnostic: if conversions are low, ask which of these is the bottleneck:
- **Motivation** (rewrite the offer and headline)
- **Ability** (remove friction: fewer form fields, lower price, simpler process)
- **Prompt** (reposition the CTA button)
